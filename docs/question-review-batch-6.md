## Yoruba

1. **Grammar and literary terms** may differ from the curriculum's wording: gbólóhùn abọ́dé / alákànpọ̀ / oníbọ̀, ọ̀rọ̀ àpọ́nlé, fáwẹ̀lì àránmúpè, fáwẹ̀lì iwájú, àrànmọ́, ìpajẹ ("kó + aṣọ → kóṣọ"), ọ̀rọ̀ àyálò, olú ẹ̀dá ìtàn, ìṣe and ìran, àfiwé tààrà, àfiwé ẹlẹ́lọ̀ọ́, ìsọhundènìyàn, àsọdùn, àwítúnwí.
2. **Customs that vary by place or source:** girls named on the 7th day; ẹkún ìyàwó most associated with Ọ̀yọ́; ọbàkan (one father, different mothers); ìrèmọ̀jẹ́ as a hunter's funeral chant; àlọ́ àpamọ̀ (riddles) vs àlọ́ àpagbè (folktales); the four-day week (Awo/Ifá, Ògún, Jàkúta/Ṣàngó, Ọ̀sẹ̀/Ọbàtálá); five nasal vowels (some books add "on").
3. **Exact wording of proverbs and idioms:** "Àìsí ológbò, ilé di ilé èkúté"; "fẹ̀jẹ̀ sínú tutọ́ funfun"; "fojú di"; "Òwe lẹṣin ọ̀rọ̀, ọ̀rọ̀ lẹṣin òwe"; "Ọmọdé gbọ́n, àgbà gbọ́n, la fi dá Ilé-Ifẹ̀"; "Àgbà kì í wà lọ́jà kí orí ọmọ títún wọ́".
4. **Tone marks** on every stem, option and the two short passages (Àyọ̀ọ́lá the farmer; the Monday market), especially àlùbáríkà, aláápọn, ẹ̀ẹ́dógún. The "which proverb sums up the passage" item depends on "Ilé ọba tó jó, ẹwà ló bù kún un" being the best fit.
5. **Mixed-language stems** (the do/re/mi vowel-pattern item and some idiom stems) may be better written fully in Yoruba.

## Islamic Religious Studies

1. **Sadl (irsal):** Nigerian Maliki practice of praying with arms at the sides (qabd is a distractor). Practice varies.
2. **Ifrad** as the preferred form of hajj in the Maliki school.
3. **Kaffarah:** "fasting 60 consecutive days", kept general because the schools differ on which broken fasts need it.
4. **"25 prophets named in the Qur'an"**, the standard school figure.
5. **Ad-Duha:** the reason for revelation (a pause in revelation) is simplified.
6. **Al-Kawthar** as the shortest surah; al-'Asr and an-Nasr (also three verses) are left out of the options.
7. **Hadith of Jibril:** narrator given as Umar (Sahih Muslim); al-Bukhari's version is from Abu Hurairah.
8. **West African names and dates:** Mai Humme Jilmi (about 1085), Ali Yaji and the Wangarawa (Kano Chronicle), al-Maghili and Muhammad Rumfa, Tabkin Kwotto (1804), the hijrah to Gudu, Jibril ibn Umar as Usman dan Fodio's teacher, al-Kanemi's letters to Muhammad Bello. Spellings vary between textbooks.
9. **Jerusalem's surrender to Umar:** about 16 AH / 637–638 CE; sources differ by a year.
10. **Transliteration** (Qur'an, ahadith, 'iddah, khul', Isra') should match DoLearnn's other IRS material.
